A good property film does something a brochure cannot: it walks a buyer through the flat, shows
how the light falls in the living room at five in the evening, and puts the building in its
neighbourhood from the air. It is often the first "visit" a buyer makes.

It is also an advertisement under the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 — RERA —
and that brings rules that a lot of property videos quietly break. Here are the rules, then the
craft.

## What RERA requires of a property advertisement

**Registration comes first.** Section 3 of the Act prohibits a promoter from advertising,
marketing, booking or selling in a real estate project without registering it with the state
authority, subject to small-project exemptions (broadly, land up to 500 square metres or up to
eight apartments). A film for an unregistered project that falls within the Act is not a
marketing asset. It is a contravention.

**Every advertisement carries two details.** Section 11(2) requires that any advertisement or
prospectus prominently mentions the **website address of the authority** and the project's **RERA
registration number**.

**Odisha adds a specific rule for video.** Odisha RERA's operational guidelines on advertising
(Circular No. 2132, 27 July 2021) require that any advertisement on TV, radio or digital media
mentions the registration number **in a clearly audible manner**, along with the ORERA website
address — [rera.odisha.gov.in](https://rera.odisha.gov.in). Print materials must show both details
prominently and in bold; hoardings must show them legibly.

In plain terms: for a property film used as an ad in Odisha, the number on the end card is not
enough on its own. The voiceover has to say it.

**The penalties are real.** Contravening the Act's provisions can attract a penalty of up to five
percent of the estimated cost of the project under Section 61. That is a large number to risk over
a missing line on an end card.

## What that means for the edit

- **Every version is an advertisement.** The 2-minute walkthrough, the 30-second cut and the
  15-second reel each need the registration number and the website. Instagram and YouTube count as
  digital media.
- **Put it in the voiceover script from the first draft**, not as an afterthought. A number read
  out takes four or five seconds; budget for them in every duration.
- **Use the number exactly as it appears on the registration certificate.** Copy it; do not retype
  it from memory.
- **Phase-wise registration means phase-wise numbers.** A film showing Phase 2 needs Phase 2's
  number.

## Show what is there

Section 12 of the Act entitles a buyer who suffers loss because of a false or incorrect statement
in an advertisement to compensation from the promoter. For a film, that turns ordinary honesty into
a legal requirement:

- **Label renders.** An architectural visualisation shown as if it were the finished building
  should carry "artist's impression" on screen while it is visible.
- **Film the actual show flat**, not a better-dressed one somewhere else.
- **Mind the lens.** Ultra-wide lenses make rooms look larger than they are. A slightly wide lens is
  normal; distortion that misrepresents a room's size is a false statement in moving pictures.
- **Amenities that are not built yet** are shown as plans or renders, labelled as such — not
  implied by footage from another project.

## The craft of a walkthrough

- **A gimbal, moving slowly.** Buyers are looking at the space, not the camera move. Slow, level,
  continuous motion lets them.
- **Walk the route a buyer would.** Entrance, living, kitchen, bedrooms, balcony — in the order a
  visit would take them.
- **Shoot interiors when the light is kind**, and exteriors at the ends of the day, when the
  building has shape rather than flat midday glare.
- **Sound is part of the pitch.** A quiet flat is a selling point; capture it rather than covering
  everything with music.

## Aerials: location, and progress

Two different jobs are done from the air:

- **Location context** — the project's relationship to the highway, the school, the lake. Checked
  against drone airspace rules before it is promised; Bhubaneswar's airport puts parts of the city
  in controlled airspace.
- **Construction progress** — the same flight path, month after month, so buyers who booked at the
  foundation stage can watch their building rise. It is one of the most effective trust-building
  assets a developer can publish, and one of the cheapest to keep producing once the route is set.
