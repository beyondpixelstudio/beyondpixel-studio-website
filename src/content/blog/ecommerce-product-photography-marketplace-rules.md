A marketplace main image is not a creative brief. It is a specification, and the platform
enforces it. An image that breaks the rules can be suppressed from search, and a listing without
a compliant main image may not go live at all.

The good news is that a specification can be met every time, if the shoot is set up for it. Here
is what the rules ask, and how a catalogue shoot is built to hit them across hundreds of SKUs.

## What Amazon asks of a main image

Amazon's requirements for the main image — the one shown in search results — are the strictest of
the large marketplaces, and the most widely copied:

- **A pure white background**, RGB 255, 255, 255. Not off-white, not light grey.
- **The product fills at least 85% of the frame.**
- **At least 1,000 pixels on the longest side**, which enables the zoom feature buyers use to
  inspect detail.
- **The actual product**, photographed — not an illustration, a render or a placeholder.
- **Nothing else**: no text, logos, watermarks, inset images or props that are not included in the
  sale.
- **JPEG preferred**; PNG, TIFF and non-animated GIF are also accepted.

Secondary images are more flexible: lifestyle shots, scale, detail and infographic panels with
text are allowed there. The strictness is concentrated on the one image that appears in search.

## Flipkart and other Indian marketplaces

Flipkart also requires a **white background** for the main image, with the product centred and a
**square** format expected. Published guidance on minimum pixel dimensions varies between sources,
so check the current figure in your seller dashboard before a shoot, and shoot comfortably above
it. The same applies to Myntra, Meesho and others: each has its own rules for background, ratio
and what may appear, and they change.

The practical approach is to shoot once at high resolution to the strictest spec — pure white,
product filling the frame, generous margins for a square crop — and export per platform.

## Pure white is made, not found

A white wall photographed is grey. Pure white is created with light:

- **The background is lit separately and brighter than the product**, so it records as true white
  without spilling onto the product's edges.
- **Shadows are a decision.** Some platforms accept a soft natural shadow under the product; others
  do not. Decide per platform before shooting, not in retouching.
- **Reflective products** — jewellery, glass, polished metal, glossy packaging — reflect
  everything around them. They need diffusion, a light tent or carefully placed flags so that what
  they reflect is clean.

## Why a studio pays for itself on a catalogue

For one product, any bright room will do. For a catalogue, consistency is the whole job, and it is
what a controlled studio buys:

- **The light does not move.** SKU number 1 and SKU number 140 are lit identically, so the
  category page looks like one brand rather than a patchwork.
- **Tethered shooting.** Frames appear on a monitor as they are taken, so the client approves
  in real time rather than discovering a problem after the shoot.
- **Colour you can trust.** A colour reference card in the first frame of each setup keeps colour
  accurate — and colour mismatch between photo and product is one of the reasons buyers return
  things.

## Planning a catalogue shoot

1. **SKU list first**, with the angles each needs: front, back, side, detail, in-use.
2. **Samples prepared** — clean, steamed, labels straight, tags removed. Every minute spent fixing
  a sample on set is a minute not shooting.
3. **A naming scheme agreed**, so files arrive named to SKU and angle rather than camera numbers.
4. **Platform list agreed**, so exports are right the first time.
5. **Realistic daily volume.** Simple packaged goods move fast; apparel on a mannequin, jewellery
  and anything reflective move slowly. Plan the day around the hardest items.
