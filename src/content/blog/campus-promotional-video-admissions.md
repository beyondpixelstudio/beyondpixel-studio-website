A campus film is watched twice. Once by the student, deciding whether this is somewhere they
want to spend three or four years. Then again by the parent, deciding whether it is somewhere
they are willing to pay for. The two are looking for different things, and a film that only
speaks to one of them usually loses the other.

It is also the one piece of institutional video with a hard deadline set by someone else: the
admissions calendar.

## Shoot on the academic calendar, not the marketing one

The most common mistake is commissioning the admissions film when admissions start. By then it
is too late to shoot it well.

Across India, entrance results and counselling rounds cluster between spring and late summer,
which is when families compare institutions most intensely. A film that goes live in July is
arriving as many decisions are being made. Work backwards:

- **Film while the campus is full.** The weeks in the academic year when students are on campus
  and not sitting exams — classes running, labs in use, clubs meeting. An empty campus in the
  vacation looks like exactly that.
- **Avoid exam weeks.** Nobody wants a camera in a hostel during end-semesters, and nobody on
  camera looks relaxed.
- **Plan exteriors around the weather.** In Odisha the monsoon runs from roughly June to
  September; grey skies and wet paths are not the campus you want to show.
- **Deliver before the window opens**, so the film is live on the website, YouTube and social when
  families start searching — not halfway through counselling.

## What to film: answer the questions families ask

Parents and students ask the same questions at every admissions counter. The film should answer
them before they ask:

- **Teaching.** Real classes in progress, not staged lectures to empty rows.
- **Labs and facilities in use** — equipment with students at it, not a pan across a locked room.
- **Where students live and eat.** Hostels and the canteen are among the most-asked-about places
  on any campus, and almost always the least filmed.
- **Outcomes.** Placements, internships, alumni. A graduate saying where they went next is worth
  more than a claim on a title card.
- **The place itself.** The approach, the green spaces, the evening on campus — what it feels like
  to be there.

## Who goes on camera

Real students and faculty, with consent. They are more convincing than models, and prospective
students can tell the difference immediately.

- **Consent in writing**, including where the film will be used and for how long.
- **Parental consent for anyone under 18** — which covers most school campuses and some first-year
  undergraduates.
- **A spread that reflects the institution** — departments, years, backgrounds — not the same
  three confident students in every shot.
- **Faculty who can speak plainly** about what their department does, in a sentence a
  sixteen-year-old understands.

## Aerials: scale, with a caveat

A campus seen from above communicates scale in seconds: the buildings, the grounds, the distance
from the city. It is usually the opening shot, and it is worth having.

The caveat is airspace. Several campuses around Bhubaneswar sit near the airport or other
restricted areas, where drone flights need clearance or are not possible at all. That has to be
checked against the exact location before an aerial is promised — see our guide to
[drone permissions in Odisha](/blog/drone-permissions-odisha-dgca-rules/).

## One shoot, many films

An institution rarely needs just one film. From the same shoot days:

- **A two-to-three-minute hero film** for the website and presentations.
- **A 60-second cut** for social and paid campaigns.
- **Department films**, one per school or programme.
- **Vertical reels** for Instagram and YouTube Shorts, where most prospective students actually
  are. These need framing that leaves room to crop, decided at the shoot.
- **Faculty and alumni interviews** that can be released through the year.

The convocation is content too — it is the proof that students finish. If yours is streamed, the
recording gives the admissions team some of its most persuasive footage.

## A planning checklist

- Admissions launch date known, and the shoot scheduled at least a couple of months before it.
- Shoot days in term time, outside exam weeks.
- Consent forms ready, including parental consent where needed.
- Hostels, canteen, labs and placement cell on the shot list.
- Airspace checked before aerials are promised.
- The full list of cuts and formats agreed before the shoot.
